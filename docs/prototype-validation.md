# Prototype and validation plan

No tests below have been executed. Numerical acceptance thresholds should be chosen for the actual material, hardware, and projector before treating a prototype as production-ready.

## 1. Fit and finish coupon

Cut a labelled range of clearances around measured stock thickness. Measure the actual cut and record laser settings and compensation location. Test unfinished and intended finished surfaces.

Select a repeatable hand-assembly fit without hammering or uncontrolled play. Document char removal, sanding, or masking if needed. Avoid double kerf compensation.

## 2. Captured joint

Build a rib/spine intersection plus its proposed closing member from 6 mm stock. Show how the joint is inserted, which movements are blocked, and how the closure is retained by the backplate interface.

Test repeated assembly, wear, withdrawal, access with neighbouring ribs, and resistance to unintended release. Inspect slot ends and remaining ligaments. Record actual outcomes, not assumed load ratings.

## 3. Complete arm and root

Build paired spines, several interchangeable ribs, end/root bracing, closure, and backing attachment.

Verify:
- Assembly order is physically possible.
- Every relevant rib extraction motion is blocked.
- The module can be removed and carried to a bench without pieces falling out.
- Backplate fasteners are reachable.
- Reconfiguration uses the intended slots without modifying parts.
- The assembly has adequate stiffness for the intended mapping.
- Root rotation, torsion, and sag are measured under representative loading.

Select loads and limits from the actual installation; do not invent a universal rating.

## 4. Intertwined arrangement

Place multiple modules in an integrated composition. Check static clearances and actual extraction paths within the proposed envelope. Prefer independent removal; otherwise record the removal sequence.

Verify the visual result reads as one sculpture and that any boundary discontinuities are intentional.

## 5. Projection and finish

Project alignment grids, narrow edge patterns, colour patches, and geometry-aware effects onto a module. Compare natural wood and selected coloured/neon finishes under intended ambient light.

Record brightness, colour limitations, occlusion, visible spill, and alignment error. Detach/reconfigure/reattach and repeat calibration checks. Fluorescence under separate UV lighting must be evaluated separately.

## 6. Software acceptance

- Same input/version produces equivalent geometry and stable identities.
- All cut contours are valid and correctly scaled.
- Mesh transforms and manifest agree with the built assembly.
- Inventory mode rejects unavailable or incompatible assignments.
- Generated assets successfully enter the chosen Arena workflow.
- Mapping is applied exactly once.
- Effect paths can cross module boundaries.
- A revised physical part receives a new definition revision.
- Reconfiguration updates projection assets without silently resizing inventory.

## Evidence

Keep machine/material settings, measured thickness, selected clearance, photographs, assembly sequence, observed failures, and revisions together. Upload only original or authorized imagery. Do not label cut files production-ready until the relevant physical checks pass.
